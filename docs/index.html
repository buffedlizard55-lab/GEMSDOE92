This session's lane: BREAKLINE (linear DEM scarp-break coherence)

Hypothesis. Quaternary fault scarps express in detrended topography as linear breaks-in-slope. The catalogue (USGS Qfaults + INGENIOUS) maps the conspicuous scarps of older imagery, and the parent's H19-5 skeleton follows potential-field (magnetic/gravity) contrasts — so a fault that juxtaposes like lithologies, or offsets only alluvium, has a scarp but no skeleton line. The live labels are newly expert-mapped faults (DrivenData #306 problem description), and experts map from high-resolution DEMs — they trace exactly this expression. Detector: L = pct_rank(||grad det_elev_slope||) × structure-tensor coherence(det_elev) (Bands 19 and 12). Full lane ranking and falsified alternatives: HYPOTHESES_20261024_BREAKLINE.md.

Mechanism. Hold count at 37,654. Keep dots: exact marginal expected-DTI greedy over R0 pixels under w = 0.30 + 0.70·L_pct, so coverage-redundant parent dots donate. Far dots: descend L with the lineage 2.8 px spacing floor over pixels > 3 px from every H19-5 pixel and > 2 px from the catalogue — concentrating on the strongest coherent breaks first. (Review measured that the exact expected-DTI greedy, spreading to cover low-rank mass, scores worse for the far stage: paired Δ +0.000701 [+0.000321, +0.001161] for spaced top-L. The greedy-emitted first builds are preserved in the repository as SUPERSEDED evidence.)

Named non-fault mimic. Pleistocene Lake Lahontan shorelines and wave-cut terraces, alluvial-fan segment boundaries (toe/piedmont junctions), and incised ephemeral-channel or canyon-rim breaks: all are natural, linear, coherent slope breaks with no fault.

Why parent 0.2778 is high — and what beating it requires
