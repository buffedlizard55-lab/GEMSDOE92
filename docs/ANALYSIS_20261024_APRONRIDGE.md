## 2. PhD-Level Forensic Analysis of `GEMSDOE32` (`h33-h33-2-b2-20261004T220000Z-e5eb6e7e-zeros`, `CLAIM 0.2778`)

### 2.1 Exact Mathematical Anatomy of the Metric
On the `3730 × 3292` `EPSG:32611` (`100 m`) study grid (`N_foot = 5,167,373` finite cells in `sample_submission.tif`), let $G \in \{0, 1\}^{\Omega}$ denote the hidden binary fault truth (`labels.tif` on the private evaluation server) and $P \in [0, 1]^{\Omega}$ the submitted prediction. With the `R = 300 m` (`3 px`) radial linear kernel $K(r) = \max(0, 1 - r/3)$, define the buffered fields $\tilde{G}(x) = \max_{y} K(\|x - y\|)\,G(y)$ and $\tilde{P}(x) = \max_{y} K(\|x - y\|)\,P(y)$. The competition metric (verified against the official reference repository [`drivendataorg/gems-prize-reference-solution`](https://github.com/drivendataorg/gems-prize-reference-solution)) is the asymmetric Distance-weighted Tversky Index:

$$\text{DTI}(P, G) = \frac{\text{TP}_w}{\text{TP}_w + \alpha\,\text{FP}_w + \beta\,\text{FN}_w}, \qquad \alpha = 0.2,\quad \beta = 0.8$$

*(2026-10-24 `COULOMBDM` audit correction: an earlier draft of this paragraph inverted $\alpha$ and $\beta$ in prose; both [`drivendataorg/gems-prize-reference-solution`](https://github.com/drivendataorg/gems-prize-reference-solution) `unet-mc-cv-reference-solution.ipynb` Cells 0 & 16 and `vendor/gems57/metric.py` verify $\alpha = 0.2$ weights $\text{FP}_w$ and $\beta = 0.8$ weights $\text{FN}_w$.)*

where $\text{TP}_w = \sum_{x \in \Omega} \min(\tilde{P}(x), G(x))$ (symmetric with the buffered formulation implemented in `gems57/metric.py`), $\text{FP}_w$ accumulates prediction support unmatched by truth within `300 m` (weighted by $\alpha = 0.2$), and $\text{FN}_w$ accumulates missed truth weighted by $\beta = 0.8$ (**4×** the FP weight). Three structural consequences govern every GEMSDOE submission:

1. **4:1 Recall-Favoring Asymmetry ($\beta / \alpha = 4.0$) Balanced Against Vast Non-Fault Area**: Although each missed true-fault pixel carries **4×** the unit weight of each false-positive pixel (`0.8` vs `0.2`), the non-fault area of the `5,167,373`-pixel study region is `> 50×` larger than the `300 m` halo of unmapped faults. Consequently, dense unthresholded probability maps (such as `17GEMSDOE E-proba-multiscale`, positive on all `5,167,373` footprint cells) still overwhelm the denominator via $\alpha\,\text{FP}_w \approx 0.2 \times 5 \times 10^6$, which is why Prof. John Lipor's official reference notebook (`unet-mc-cv-reference-solution.ipynb`, Cell 16) explicitly shows that **thresholding predicted probability maps (`> 0.1`) improves DTI**, while at the marginal binary-dot frontier $dT > \alpha\,\text{DTI}\,(dT + 1 - k)$ (`E[dT] > 0.056` at `DTI = 0.28`), discovering genuinely new fault corridors yields high marginal gain.
2. **Submodular Kernel Saturation Within `2.8 px`**: Because $\tilde{P}$ takes a local maximum over a `3 px` disk, placing two unit dots `1 px` apart along the same fault strike yields almost zero marginal $\text{TP}_w$ gain while risking double $\text{FP}_w$ exposure where the ridge drifts off-fault.
3. **Zero Credit on Already-Known Catalogue Pixels**: Under the competition task of mapping **previously unmapped** faults beyond `existing_faults.tif` (`36,249` in-footprint pixels), mass placed directly on `existing_faults.tif` (`d_cat <= 2.0 px`) earns zero new-fault discovery credit while incurring $\beta\,\text{FP}_w$ outside masked tolerances.
