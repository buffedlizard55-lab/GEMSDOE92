3. Why h33-h33-2-b2 scores 0.2778 — the metric algebra, not a story

With the official metric (α = 0.2, β = 0.8, 300 m triangular kernel; transcribed in vendor/gems57/metric.py) and writing T = covered truth credit, M = dot self-credit, n = dot count, K = truth mass:

DTI = T / ( α(T + n − M) + βK ). The denominator does not collapse to αn + βK — the shared template's own docstring flags that shortcut as valid only when T == M.

The five lineage claims form a subset chain (d2.8 ⊇ h27-4 ⊇ h33: 44,090 → 40,199 → 37,654 dots, verified by byte check). The three claims identify T from their differences alone, with no assumption about K or M: T ≈ 5,073 (d2.8→h27-4), 5,223 (d2.8→h33), 5,470 (h27-4→h33) — and the spread is itself informative: each pruning step costs a little real coverage, not only dead weight. K and M are not separately identifiable from three claims (only the combination β·K − α·M is); assuming K = 13,219 (the value fitted by scripts/gx_estimator.py) gives M ≈ 1,744 — i.e. the 0.2778 file covers about 40 % of the scored truth mass with roughly 10 % of its dots (≈ 3,900 of 37,654) carrying essentially all of the credit. Three consequences, all of them actionable:

  1. Thinning pays. Dropping a dot costs 0.2 of denominator and only its own yield. The 0.2600 → 0.2708 → 0.2778 steps are exactly this: 3,891 then 2,545 catalogue-adjacent dots removed at ~zero yield cost.
  2. False negatives dominate. βK ≈ 10,575 is 56 % of the denominator and α(n − M) ≈ 7,183 is 38 %. Coverage T is only 5.6 %. The leaderboard top (0.4544 on the 2026-10-30 read) implies ≈ 67 % coverage (T ≈ 8,875) at a similar dot budget — i.e. the gap is information, not tuning.
  3. Pruning is the largest single lever available without new data. Half of h33's dots are pure cost; removing 18,000 zero-yield dots at constant T would give ≈ 0.34. Doing that needs a per-dot yield ranking, which is the untested experiment proposed in §6.
