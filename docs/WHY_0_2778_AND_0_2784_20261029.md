## 1. Exact Mathematical Anatomy of the Distance-Buffered Tversky Index (DTI)

Let $\Omega \subset \{0,\dots,3729\}\times\{0,\dots,3291\}$ (`EPSG:32611`, $100\text{ m}$ cells, $|\Omega| = 5{,}167{,}373$) be the valid competition footprint, $F \subset \Omega$ (`|F| = 60,988` pixels) be the public USGS/INGENIOUS fault catalogue (`existing_faults.tif`), and $G \subset \Omega \setminus F$ be the hidden test fault trace set. Per official DrivenData Forum clarifications (`#11516`, `#11550`, `#11536`), pixels in $F$ are masked pixel-exactly during evaluation, and DTI pools weighted true positives, false positives, and false negatives across $\Omega \setminus F$ with triangular distance kernel $k(d) = \max(0, 1 - d / 300\text{ m})$ ($R = 3\text{ px}$) and Tversky weights $(\alpha, \beta) = (0.2, 0.8)$:

$$\mathrm{TP}_w(p) = \sum_{g \in G} \max_{x \in \Omega \setminus F} p(x)\,k(\|x - g\|_2), \qquad \mathrm{FP}_w(p) = \sum_{x \in \Omega \setminus F} p(x)\Bigl(1 - \max_{g \in G} k(\|x - g\|_2)\Bigr), \qquad \mathrm{FN}_w(p) = |G| - \mathrm{TP}_w(p),$$

$$\mathrm{DTI}(p) = \frac{\mathrm{TP}_w(p)}{\mathrm{TP}_w(p) + 0.2\,\mathrm{FP}_w(p) + 0.8\,\mathrm{FN}_w(p)} = \frac{\mathrm{TP}_w(p)}{0.8\,|G| + 0.2\,\mathrm{TP}_w(p) + 0.2\,\mathrm{FP}_w(p)}.$$

Three structural consequences follow immediately from Differentiating $\mathrm{DTI}$ with respect to adding a binary dot $p(x_0)=1$ at location $x_0 \in \Omega \setminus F$:

1. **Submodularity of $\mathrm{TP}_w$ vs. Strict Linearity of $\mathrm{FP}_w$ (Why Poisson-Disk Thinning Beats Continuous Ribbons):**
   Because $\mathrm{TP}_w$ applies a **hard $\max_{x}$ operator** over the 29-pixel disk $\|x - g\|_2 < 3\text{ px}$, a second dot placed $1\text{ px}$ ($100\text{ m}$) from an existing dot on the same fault trace increments $\max_x k(\|x-g\|_2)$ by at most $\Delta k \le 1/3$ on a handful of pixels while paying the **full linear false-positive penalty** $0.2\,(1 - K_G(x_0))$ whenever $x_0$ lies off $G$. Starting from the continuous $1\text{ px}$ skeleton `H19-5` (`121,131` pixels, `CLAIM 0.1922`), Poisson-disk thinning at $d_{\min} = 1.5\text{ px}$ (`60,069` dots, `CLAIM 0.2477`) and $d_{\min} = 2.8\text{ px}$ (`44,090` dots, `CLAIM 0.2600`) eliminated ~`63.6%` of $\mathrm{FP}_w$ mass while retaining $>91\%$ of the $300\text{ m}$ triangular roof coverage along the same linework (`evidence/r0_forensics_20261024.json`).
2. **Asymmetric Recall Bias ($\beta/\alpha = 4$) and Optimal Emission Threshold $\tau^\star$:**
   Adding dot $x_0$ with incremental coverage gain $\Delta \mathrm{TP}_w(x_0)$ and expected false-positive cost $1 - \bar{k}_G(x_0)$ increases $\mathrm{DTI}$ if and only if:
   $$\frac{\Delta \mathrm{TP}_w(x_0)}{\Delta \mathrm{TP}_w(x_0) + \Delta \mathrm{FP}_w(x_0)} > \frac{0.2\,\mathrm{DTI}}{1 - 0.8\,\mathrm{DTI}}.$$
   At $\mathrm{DTI} \approx 0.28$, the break-even marginal precision per unit mass is **only $\tau^\star = \frac{0.056}{0.776} \approx 0.0722$** ($7.22\%$). That $4:1$ recall-to-precision asymmetry explains why ultra-conservative budgets (`< 15,000` dots) suffer severe $\mathrm{FN}_w$ penalties, while well-spaced budgets in the `35,000–45,000` dot regime maximize the ratio.
3. **Near-Catalogue Collar (`d_cat > 2 px`, `200 m`) and Map-Generalization Halo:**
   USGS Quaternary and INGENIOUS compilation traces ($F$) exhibit $100\text{–}250\text{ m}$ digitization offset relative to 3DEP topographic scarps. In `H19-5`, `6,435` of the `44,090` $2.8\text{ px}$-spaced dots lay at $0 < d_{\text{cat}} \le 2\text{ px}$ — parallel twin detections of **already-catalogued** faults in $F$ rather than genuinely unmapped faults in $G$. ## 1. Mathematical Anatomy of the DrivenData DOE GEMS Distance-Tolerant IoU (`DTI`) Metric

Let $\Omega \subset \{1,\dots,3730\}\times\{1,\dots,3292\}$ denote the active `5,167,373`-pixel Great Basin footprint (`100 m × 100 m` cells in `EPSG:32611`), $C \subset \Omega$ the `60,988`-pixel mapped fault compilation (`existing_faults.tif`, masked to zero before scoring), and $T^{\star} \subset \Omega \setminus C$ the hidden unmapped Quaternary/geothermal fault pixel set of total mass $G = |T^{\star}|$.

With the `300 m` (`3.0 px`) linear triangular distance kernel $K_r(d) = \max(0,\, 1 - d / 3.0)$, a candidate raster $p : \Omega \setminus C \to [0, 1]$ generates:
1. **Predicted coverage field** $C_p(x) = \max_{y \in \Omega \setminus C} p(y)\,K_r(\|x - y\|_2)$, so **true-positive mass** is:
   $$\text{TP}_w(p) = \sum_{x \in T^{\star}} C_p(x)$$
2. **False-positive mass** weighted against true coverage $C_{T^{\star}}(y) = \max_{x \in T^{\star}} K_r(\|y - x\|_2)$:
   $$\text{FP}_w(p) = \sum_{y \in \Omega \setminus C} p(y)\,\big(1 - C_{T^{\star}}(y)\big)$$
3. **Asymmetric Distance-Tolerant IoU** (`alpha = 0.2`, `beta = 0.8`):
   $$\text{DTI}(p) = \frac{\text{TP}_w(p)}{0.2\,\text{TP}_w(p) + 0.8\,G + 0.2\,\text{FP}_w(p)}$$

Taking the Fréchet / marginal pixel increment $\Delta \text{DTI}(y)$ when setting pixel $y$ from $0 \to 1$ at current score level $D = \text{DTI}(p)$ yields the **exact marginal optimality threshold**:
$$\Delta \text{DTI}(y) > 0 \iff \frac{\Delta \text{TP}_w(y)}{\Delta \text{FP}_w(y)} > \frac{0.2\,D}{1 - 0.2\,D}$$
At $D = 0.2784$, the break-even marginal ratio is:
$$\tau^{\star}(0.2784) = \frac{0.2 \times 0.2784}{1 - 0.2 \times 0.2784} = \mathbf{0.05896}$$

Three structural consequences follow immediately from $\tau^{\star} \approx 0.059$:
1. **Why Continuous Probability Surfaces Collapse (`0.0206 – 0.1647` `CLAIM`):** Any raster that emits unthresholded background probability $p(y) \in (0.01, 0.25)$ across millions of basin/range cells (`GEMSDOE28 s18-mxtp: 0.0206`, `GEMSDOE36 submission_p97: 0.1647`, `17GEMSDOE E-proba-multiscale: 0.1560`) accumulates $10^5$–$10^6$ units of $0.2\,\text{FP}_w$ denominator drag without increasing $\max_y p(y) K_r(\|x-y\|)$ on fault corridors already covered.
2. **Why `2.5–3.0 px` Sparse Crest Dotting Gives a `1.44×` Boost (`0.1922 -> 0.2778` `CLAIM`):** A continuous 1-px-wide skeleton line places 3–5 adjacent `1.0` pixels inside a single `3 px` (`300 m`) kernel disk. The second, third, and fourth pixels add almost **zero** marginal $\Delta \text{TP}_w$ (because $\max_y K_r$ is submodular and already saturated near $1.0$), while paying **full linear** $0.2\,(1 - C_{T^{\star}})$ false-positive cost! Thinning a line to isolated `1.0` dots spaced by `2.6–3.0 px` preserves $>91\%$ of corridor coverage $\text{TP}_w$ while cutting $\text{FP}_w$ by $\sim 60$–$70\%$.
3. **Why Near-Catalogue Collar Pruning (`d_cat > 2–4 px`) Helps:** Cells at $d_{\text{cat}} \le 2\text{ px}$ (`200 m`) sit inside the digitization uncertainty halo of already-masked `existing_faults.tif` traces; unmapped *new* strands overwhelmingly occupy synthetic/antithetic piedmont step-overs, relay ramps, and intra-basin grabens at $d_{\text{cat}} > 3$–$15\text{ px}$.
