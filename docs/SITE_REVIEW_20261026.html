Why GEMSDOE32 / R0 is the best reference among these claims

The public GEMSDOE32 h33-h33-2-b2 zero-outside raster matches the locally pinned R0 bytes (SHA-256 c55bafc470054e8271dcb89347a17e07fefe50de6af6e6ba6c4b169ef7ab6fa9). Verified lineage: Exact local parent R0 file; verified set lineage H19-5 (121,131) -> D2.8 (44,090) -> H27-4 SOLO (40,199) -> R0 (37,654). R0 is exactly D2.8 & distance-to-catalogue > 2 px; SOLO is exactly D2.8 & distance > 1 px.

Plausible mechanism, not causal proof: under the project's vendored DTI definition, a 300 m triangular kernel weights false positives by α=0.2 and false negatives by β=0.8. Thinning can remove redundant false-positive mass while retaining nearby truth credit; a >2-pixel catalogue collar can avoid spending predictions on mapped faults. Hidden truth and an organizer score-to-file receipt are absent, so this is a mechanistic explanation only.

Methods diverge from the denser 1.5-pixel H19-5 thinning (GEMSDOE24), the gap-closure extension (GEMSDOE27), the 1-pixel catalogue collar (GEMSDOE31), the exact R0 2-pixel collar (GEMSDOE32), and the Anderson PINN superset (GEMSDOE36). GEMSDOE25 and GEMSDOE30 are identical in-footprint masks despite different file hashes. The complete identities and measurements are in the JSON.
