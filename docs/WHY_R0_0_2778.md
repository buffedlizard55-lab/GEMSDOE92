## 1. Short answer

1. **What R0 is (MEASURED).** A binary 0/1 dot raster of 37,654 pixels: the H19-5 magnetic/gravity
   lineament skeleton (121,131 px) thinned to a ≥ 2.83 px spacing (44,090 dots), then stripped of every dot
   within 2 px of the mapped catalogue. The nesting R0 ⊂ SOLO (40,199) ⊂ D2.8 (44,090) ⊂ H19-5 is exact.
2. **Why it scores more than its siblings (MODEL on CLAIMs).** Its gain over the previous best (0.2708) comes
   from deleting 2,545 dots at 1.41–2.0 px from mapped faults that the scorer treats as pure false
   positives; the gain per deleted dot (2.75e-6) sits at 95 % of the zero-credit ceiling (2.89e-6).
   Every *other* tested change either re-selects skeleton dots (score unchanged), or adds dots from another source
   at credit below break-even (score lower), or is an independent lane with lower credit per dot.
3. **Can it be beaten?** In principle yes — the public board already shows far higher scores — *but not by tuning R0's own rules*. The public leaderboard
   snapshot fetched today shows #1 at 0.4413 and 50 rows at ≥ 0.2818, so 0.2778 is outside the visible
   top 50. Inside R0's lane the remaining headroom is ≈ +0.01 (MODEL); the large headroom is in **ranking
   precision** (R0 recovers ≈ 40 % of the truth but ≈ 95 % of its emitted mass is counted as false positive — MODEL), which
   needs information R0 does not contain.
4. **The two cited highs.** The prompt quotes 0.4413 and elsewhere 0.3195. The live page shows 0.4413 at #1
   (xiaofanhu, 29 submissions); 0.3195 is an older snapshot that cannot be checked from here. Note: 0.4413 is
   numerically identical (4 d.p.) to GEMSDOE32's own *live-mirror model output* for a 7,943-dot variant
   (0.44127926, `evidence/h33_removal_rule_scan.json` there). That is almost certainly a coincidence; it is
   flagged as IR-92-03 and no inference is drawn from it.

## 2. The scoring facts everything rests on (OFFICIAL)

* Metric: distance-weighted Tversky, α = 0.2 (false positives), β = 0.8 (false negatives), 300 m
  triangular kernel — [problem description](https://www.drivendata.org/competitions/306/competition-doe-gems/page/967/).
* Known USGS/INGENIOUS fault pixels are **masked / excluded** from scoring in Phase 1 *and* the same dataset is
  excluded in Phase 2 — staff replies in forum threads
  [11516](https://community.drivendata.org/t/scoring-clarification-are-known-usgs-ingenious-faults-masked-when-scoring-and-are-they-in-the-final-round-label-set/11516)
  (2026-09-16) and [11556](https://community.drivendata.org/t/questions-about-the-final-round-rules/11556) (2026-10-07).
* "New fault" = *any fault pixel not already captured by USGS/INGENIOUS*, incl. newly mapped geometry of an
  existing system — [thread 11536](https://community.drivendata.org/t/where-do-you-draw-the-line/11536).
* Leaderboard = one Tversky index pooled over all pixels of the public subset; private and final use the same
  metric, final on the whole GeoDAWN area; no extra fault weighting — threads
  [11550](https://community.drivendata.org/t/leaderboard-aggregation-pooled-over-public-test-pixels-or-mean-of-per-chunk-scores/11550),
  [11556](https://community.drivendata.org/t/questions-about-the-final-round-rules/11556).
* The truth's data sources, fault types and coverage are **not disclosed** —
  [thread 11527](https://community.drivendata.org/t/how-were-the-new-test-faults-identified-data-sources-and-fault-types/11527),
  staff reply 2026-09-23. Anything below about "what new faults look like" is inference.
* Submission = one single-band GeoTIFF at 100 m over the GeoDAWN area; three per week; the website limit
  prevails ([thread 11553](https://community.drivendata.org/t/submission-amount-question/11553));
  generative-AI use must be disclosed ([Official Rules §3.2](https://docs.nlr.gov/docs/fy26osti/96647.pdf)).

**Algebra (MODEL).** With T = Σ_truth max-kernel-credit, FP = N − M (M = kernel-closeness of the dots to truth),
DTI = T / (0.2 T + 0.2 FP + 0.8 (|G| − T)). Hence a dot with credit c and closeness k̄ raises DTI iff
c ≳ 0.2·DTI·(1 − k̄)/(1 − 0.2·DTI) ≈ **0.056** at DTI ≈ 0.28. A dot dropped uniformly at random earns
≈ (|G|/footprint)·∫kernel = **0.023**; a dot must therefore be ≥ 2.5× better than random to be worth emitting.

Zero-credit ceiling for removing one false-positive dot at these operating points: **2.89e-6** (MODEL). Both flank
groups reach 96 % / 95 % of it, i.e. they carried ≈ 0 credit (group 2: ≈ 6 units of ~5,060, 0.0025 per dot).
The two removal pairs also disagree on T by 8 % under a strict zero-credit assumption (5,073 vs 5,470), which is
what a small positive credit in group 2 predicts — a consistency check, not a flaw.

**Why those dots were worthless.** The H19-5 skeleton is *enriched next to the catalogue*: 3.2× the footprint share
at 1–2 px, 2.3× at 2–3 px, 1.5× at 4–5 px, falling to ≈ 0.7–0.8× beyond 20 px (MEASURED). The catalogue pixels
themselves are masked, but their 1–2 px neighbours are not; a skeleton ridge that re-detects a known fault one
or two pixels off is penalised in full unless a *new* fault pixel happens to lie within 300 m — and the ladder says
it did not.

| quantity | value |
|---|---:|
| truth pixels implied, \|G\| | ≈ 12,100–12,700 from the ladder under its zero-credit assumption; 11,000–14,000 allowed for that assumption |
| credit T of R0 | ≈ 4,700–5,400 (5,057 at 12,500) |
| recall (T/\|G\|) | ≈ 39–43 % |
| credit per dot | 0.125–0.144 (0.134 at 12,500) |
| vs random dot (0.023) | ≈ 5.9× |

So the skeleton is a *real but weak* detector: about six times better than random placement per dot.

## 5. Why nothing else in the GEMSDOE family beat it

All sets below are MEASURED relative to R0/SOLO; scores are CLAIMs; credit is MODEL.

| group | change vs base | score change | what it implies |
|---|---|---:|---|
| R0 + 1,200 "geothermal PINN" dots (GEMSDOE36) | +1,200 added, R0 ⊂ file | 0.2778 → 0.2750 | added dots earned ≈ 0.012 each — about half of random placement, ~5× below break-even |
| SOLO re-thinned (rung30 / hf-euler, GEMSDOE28) | ~12.3k swapped for ~10k other skeleton dots on **long** components | 0.2708 → 0.2710 / 0.2707 | exchangeable: *which* skeleton dots you keep along a line is irrelevant at first order |
| SOLO + tip/Euler dots (GEMSDOE28 prethin) | −1,246 / +3,341 | 0.2708 → 0.2649 | net +2,095 dots at zero credit (predicts −0.0059; observed −0.0059) |
| SOLO tip/step-over swap (GEMSDOE33) | −6,529 / +8,195 | 0.2708 → 0.2632 | if the removed dots earned R0-like 0.13 each, the added ones earned ≈ 0.10 (net +1,666 dots also cost FP) |
| independent lanes (GEMSDOE70, 49, 76, 63, 73, 82, 75, 78, 10) | different detectors, 37–69k dots | 0.15–0.25 | credit per dot 0.065–0.119: above break-even, below R0 |

R0 is therefore the best *skeleton* with the *right catalogue exclusion*; every alternative either shares its
information (and adds false positives) or is individually weaker. That is the whole explanation — no
hidden trick, and no evidence that its position (e.g. along-strike structure) is better than any other skeleton choice.

## 6. Can it be beaten? Where the headroom is, and is not

* **Inside R0's lane (≈ +0.01, MODEL).**
  * *Catalogue halo beyond 2 px.* Halo survival estimated from the skeleton's own enrichment puts dots at 2–3 px at
    ≈ 0.29–0.38 survival (credit ≈ 0.05–0.06 ≈ break-even): removing them is worth ≈ 0 to +0.003. Not a lever.
  * *Skeleton fragments (UNTESTED live).* **8,719 of R0's 37,654 dots (23.2 %) sit on isolated one-pixel skeleton
    components; 59.7 % on components ≤ 10 px** (H19-5 has 26,645 components, median size 2 px — MEASURED). No scored
    raster in the registry removes them: the rung30/hf-euler swaps touch long components only (their *kept* set is 30 %
    singletons). MODEL change in DTI (|G| = 12,500): **+0.029** if those dots carry zero credit, +0.019 at 0.02 per dot, ≈ 0 at the
    0.056 break-even, −0.017 at 0.09, **−0.039** if they carry R0's average 0.134 (|G| = 11,000–14,000 moves these by ≤ 0.004). It is the single highest-stakes untested experiment around R0 and is a lane-duplicate
    (R0 subset), so it needs a human-selected probe slot, not a lane slot (see `docs/NEXT_STEPS.md`).
* **Beyond it.** If one could keep exactly R0's hit dots, DTI would be ≈ 0.47 (MODEL). The leaderboard's
  0.33–0.44 region implies credit per dot of roughly 0.3–0.5. That is a *ranking-precision* gap, not a rule gap,
  and needs information that is not in R0 or in the 19 bands alone (the #5 team publicly describes 20 m
  lidar-derivative U-Net ensembles validated on public expert picks — forum thread
  [11531](https://community.drivendata.org/t/looking-for-a-geologist-teammate-lidar-fault-mapping-basin-and-range-to-join-a-working-ml-pipeline/11531),
  2026-09-20 — which this sandbox cannot reproduce: no route to USGS 3DEP).
* **What this session built from it:** the LCS lane (`docs/HYPOTHESES_20261024_LCS.md`) — a unique, matched-budget
  candidate whose dots are chosen by leaderboard-weighted agreement among independent lanes. Its MODEL projection is
  R0 ± 0.066 (one typical leave-one-lane-out model error); it is an *exploration arm*, not a claim of improvement.
