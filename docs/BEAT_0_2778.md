## Why thinning a fault skeleton could help a distance-weighted Tversky score

This is a mechanism-level explanation, not a verified account of the claimed organizer result.

For a distance-tolerant metric, several nearby predicted pixels can receive overlapping credit around the same mapped segment. If true-positive credit is capped or effectively saturates locally, removing redundant predictions may reduce weighted false-positive mass while preserving much of the proximity credit. A fixed prediction budget can also be redirected toward parts of the footprint not already well represented. Those effects make a thinned or reallocated skeleton a plausible candidate to test.

That argument is conditional. It does **not** imply that every nearby point is redundant, that more spacing always improves the score, or that a catalog-adjacent prediction cannot help recover a withheld fault. The repository has a counterexample test showing nearby dots can increase DTI. Exact effects depend on the organizer's scoring implementation, prediction budget, hidden-label geometry, and the files actually submitted.

The repository's prior analysis describes `R0` as a thinned descendant of H19-5. That lineage is useful context, but the lineage alone cannot establish why a private score would be high. The pinned GEMSDOE32 source review and its limitations are summarized in [`REVIEW_376A8469.md`](REVIEW_376A8469.md); inspect its [source audit](downloads/review376a_sources.json) before treating historical site statements as facts.

## How to answer the “why/how” question rigorously

1. Obtain an actual organizer submission-page receipt for the named GEMSDOE32 artifact. Verify file identity, score, competition round, and metric; otherwise retain `0.2778` as a claim.
2. Reproduce the exact candidate from its versioned source and byte-compare it to the named TIFF. Do not infer behavior from a similar filename or descendant.
3. Independently validate the organizer metric implementation against official materials. A local evaluator is a surrogate unless its equivalence has been demonstrated.
4. Compare the original and thinned prediction under identical spatially blocked hide-and-recover folds, budgets, withheld positives, and bootstrap procedure. Attribute gains only through paired ablations that vary one design factor at a time.
5. Keep a locked outer spatial test to reduce adaptive-selection bias from the many experiments already run.
6. Apply the literal registry overlap/correlation test before placement and after final dot selection. Under the current pinned full-footprint witness, a nonempty candidate necessarily fails the literal `0.70` forward-overlap rule; stop before experiment or slot selection.
