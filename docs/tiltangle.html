Hypothesis: why this surface targets faults missing from the catalogue

The challenge supplies existing USGS/INGENIOUS fault labels, which the organizer says are incomplete; they are not solely a catalogue of preserved surface scarps. A hypothetical buried normal fault might juxtapose rock bodies of different density without a pronounced surface scarp, producing a gravity gradient. A gradient alone does not establish a fault or geothermal activity.

The archived TILTANGLE implementation computes θ = atan2(|∂z g|, |∇ₕ g|) from bands B11 (vertical derivative), B13 (gravity anomaly, differentiated for N–S derivative), and B18 (E–W horizontal derivative). This is an angle between field-derivative magnitudes, not fault dip: θ approaches 0 when the horizontal gradient dominates and 90° when the vertical derivative dominates. The actual edge score is |∇ₕ g| × cos(θ)² = |∇ₕ g|³ / (|∇ₕ g|² + |∂z g|²), when the denominator is nonzero. It favors horizontally sharp gravity transitions; it does not prove a steep fault or discriminate a circular body from a fault. Earlier wording incorrectly described θ≈90° as a steep contact even though cos²θ then approaches zero. This is a documentation correction only; the TIFF was not regenerated. The catalogue is not directly fed to the scoring surface but is used for masking.

Named non-fault mimics: a plutonic margin, intact lithologic contact, or playa/evaporite density boundary can also produce a sharp horizontal gradient; the cos² factor does not guarantee their removal. A future study must use independent geological controls and test the possible median-fill boundary artifact before claiming a new fault.

How it differs from existing lanes: no lane in this repo computes a gravity tilt angle. GRAVQUAD uses analytic-signal amplitude × structure-tensor coherence (dip-blind). COULOMB-LOQO assumes fixed N15°E conjugate shear planes inside a supervised classifier. CURIE-DEMAG uses RTP variance suppression × MT clay-cap flank × demag bowl.
