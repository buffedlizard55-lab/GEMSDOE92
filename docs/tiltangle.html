Hypothesis: why this surface targets faults missing from the catalogue

The USGS/INGENIOUS catalogue is a surface-scarp catalogue: faults are mapped where a preserved topographic scarp exists. A blind normal fault buried under thin Quaternary cover, or one whose scarp has diffused below detection, still juxtaposes rock bodies of different density across a steep plane. That density contrast is visible to gravity regardless of scarp preservation or fault strike.

The TILTANGLE surface computes the gravity tilt angle θ = atan2(|∂z g|, |∇ₕ g|) from bands B11 (vertical derivative), B13 (gravity anomaly, differentiated for N–S derivative), and B18 (E–W horizontal derivative). θ ≈ 90° over steep near-vertical contacts (faults) and ≈ 0° over flat bedding. Edge strength = horizontal-gradient magnitude × cos(θ)², which selects steep-contact edges of any strike and down-weights equant/blob anomalies (plutons, circular density bodies). The catalogue is used only for masking — no catalogue pixel enters the surface.

Named non-fault mimic: equant plutonic intrusions and circular playa/evaporite density bodies produce horizontal-gradient magnitude but not steep tilt angles; cos(θ)² down-weights them.

How it differs from existing lanes: no lane in this repo computes a gravity tilt angle. GRAVQUAD uses analytic-signal amplitude × structure-tensor coherence (dip-blind). COULOMB-LOQO assumes fixed N15°E conjugate shear planes inside a supervised classifier. CURIE-DEMAG uses RTP variance suppression × MT clay-cap flank × demag bowl.
