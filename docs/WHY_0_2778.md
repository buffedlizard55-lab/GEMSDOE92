# Why GEMSDOE32 `h33-h33-2-b2-20261004T220000Z-e5eb6e7e-zeros` scored 0.2778, and how to beat it

*Labels: `CLAIM` = owner-reported leaderboard score (no organizer receipt in this repo); `MODEL` = this
session's metric-structured leaderboard model (`gems/lbmodel.py`, `evidence/lbm_v1_model.json`); no number
here is an organizer-confirmed score.*

## 1. The metric, exactly ([problem description](https://www.drivendata.org/competitions/306/competition-doe-gems/page/967/))

With triangular kernel `k(d) = max(0, 1 − d/3 px)` (300 m support), truth set `G`, predictions `p(x)`:

* `TPw = Σ_g max_x p(x)k(d(x,g))`
* `FPw = Σ_x p(x)[1 − max_g k(d(x,g))]`
* `FNw = |G| − TPw`

That gives `DTI = TPw / (TPw + 0.2·FPw + 0.8·FNw) = TPw / (0.2·TPw + 0.2·FPw + 0.8·|G|)`.

Three consequences drive everything below.

1. **Binary dots are optimal.** Scaling one dot's value scales its TP and FP contributions together, so the
   optimum is bang-bang. All top submissions are 0/1 dot fields.
2. **The admission bar is low.** One more non-redundant dot with self-credit `k` and marginal truth credit `ΔT`
   raises DTI iff `ΔT·(1 − 0.2·DTI) > 0.2·DTI·(1 − k)`. At DTI ≈ 0.28 that is `ΔT > 0.059·(1 − k)`: a dot needs only a few
   per cent chance of landing within 300 m of a new fault.
3. **Known faults are masked pixel-exactly.** A dot next to a mapped fault but away from new faults is fully
   penalised ([DrivenData staff, thread 11516](https://community.drivendata.org/t/scoring-clarification-are-known-usgs-ingenious-faults-masked-when-scoring-and-are-they-in-the-final-round-label-set/11516/4)).

## 2. The lineage is a controlled experiment (all `CLAIM`)

Exact set algebra, verified on the restored rasters: `R0 ≡ D2.8 ∧ d_cat > 2` and `SOLO ≡ D2.8 ∧ d_cat > 1`, both with
0-pixel XOR.

**Why thinning helped (steps 1–2).** Dots spaced `s` px along a trace give on-trace truth a mean credit of
`1 − s/12`: 0.875 at 1.5 px and 0.767 at 2.8 px. Meanwhile the positive count, which is almost all FP mass at
roughly 5% precision, falls by a factor of 2.0–2.7. The FP term (0.2·FPw) shrinks much faster than the TP term.

**Why the collar helped (steps 3–4).** The two removals gained `+0.0108/3,891 = 2.78×10⁻⁶` and
`+0.0070/2,545 = 2.75×10⁻⁶` per removed dot. Those are the same, and equal the pure-false-positive value
`0.2·DTI²/TPw`. So dots 100–200 m from mapped faults carried essentially **zero** truth credit: the
organizer's new faults are not beside mapped traces.

**Why 0.2778 and not more.** Under MODEL, R0 has kernel-weighted recall `TPw/|G| ≈ 0.37` and spends about 97% of
its dot mass as FP. That FP mass sits largely 200 m–1.5 km from mapped faults (truth-density multiplier ×0.38)
and in places where independent detectors disagree. The FP term is ~49% of the denominator.

## 3. Can it be beaten? Yes, if FP mass is moved to where truth is denser

The MODEL fit to all 123 scored rasters finds three truth-density multipliers: ×5.7 beyond 1.5 km from mapped faults,
×3.8 at exactly 1 px off the H19-5 skeleton, and ×15 where at least 5 of 10 independent high-scoring lineages agree
within 200 m. EXP1 places dots by exact expected-DTI greedy search under that density. It caps every registry raster's
3 px halo at 69% of the dots, so the result is **not a copy** of any prior submission. See the run card for the MODEL
estimate and its interval.

The user-reported leaderboard values `0.3195` and `0.4413` were not independently verified (DrivenData's terms
forbid automated leaderboard scraping). A 0.44 under this metric needs roughly half the truth covered with FP mass
about equal to `|G|`. That is much sharper localisation than any 100 m feature stack in this repo provides, and is
consistent with 1 m lidar scarp mapping (hypothesis H4).
