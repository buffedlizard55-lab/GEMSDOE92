## Why Prior Off-Skeleton Lanes Failed to Beat `RELIEF p10` (0.006045) on Holdout

A diagnostic audit of the pinned parent lineage (`H19-5` → `D2.8` → `SOLO` → `R0`) and prior off-skeleton lanes (`BREAKLINE`, `SCARPPOL`, `ANASIG`, `STRAINBREAK`, `SPRINGALIGN`) identified two coupled structural-geological and algorithmic defects:

1. **Mountain-interior bias of unnormalized slope-break detectors:**
   `BREAKLINE` (`gems/breakline.py`), `SCARPPOL` (`gems/scarppol.py`), and `STRAINBREAK` (`gems/strainbreak.py`) computed the *unnormalized* gradient of detrended-elevation slope `‖∇det_elev_slope‖` (Band 19). Because raw slope in steep bedrock mountain ranges reaches 20°–45° while alluvial fans slope at 1°–6°, unnormalized `‖∇det_elev_slope‖` placed **74.8% of `BREAKLINE f45`'s off-skeleton dots inside high-elevation mountain range interiors** (`det_elev ≥ p67`, median slope 19.28°, median basement depth 77.8 m) and only **18.2% in the piedmont tercile**. While those mountain-interior dots scored well on the USGS SGMC bedrock fault map (`0.12611`), they missed the low-relief Quaternary alluvial fault scarps and range-front step-faults along the piedmont apron and fell short of `RELIEF p10` (`0.006045`) on `gems57-pooled-hide-v2`.
2. **Post-thinning `b2` deletion holes on the `H19-5` skeleton:**
   Exact comparison of `D2.8`, `SOLO`, and `R0` proved that `R0 == D2.8 & (d_cat > 2.0) == SOLO & (d_cat > 2.0)` byte-for-byte on the dot mask. Because `D2.8` applied 2.83-px Poisson-disk thinning *before* `R0` deleted the 6,436 dots with `d_cat ≤ 2.0 px`, those 6,436 deleted dots cast a 2.83-px exclusion shadow onto adjacent legal `H19-5` strand pixels (`2.0 < d_cat ≤ 4.83 px`) and then vanished, leaving **794 spacing-legal holes (`d_r0 > 2.8 px`)** and **5,383 kernel-undercovered skeleton pixels (`d_r0 > 2.0 px`)** where `H19-5` splays emerge from the catalogue exclusion. Every prior off-skeleton lane restricted its keep stage to `cand_mask = r0.copy()`, inheriting `R0`'s holes and sharing ~90% of kept dots (`docs/NEXT_STEPS.md` item 2).
