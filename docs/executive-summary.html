Submission procedure (only after a separate eligibility clearance)

Do not upload the current downloadable files. For a future artifact, first verify that its own run card says verdict: promote, its pre-placement and final-dot registry checks pass the explicitly applicable policy, its holdout result beats the current comparable holdout best, and its audit passes every format gate. Slot selection and the DrivenData upload are separate from this workbench; only then:

  1. Download the exact zeros-outside .tif file specified by the cleared run card. Do not edit or re-save it in GIS software.
  2. Sign into the official DrivenData competition and open its new-submission form. The portal requests a single-band GeoTIFF or a ZIP containing one GeoTIFF.
  3. Select that cleared file in “File to submit”. Paste the run-card note into “Note (optional)”. The submission name is a project/ledger identifier; the described portal form has no separate name field, so do not assume one exists.
  4. After an accepted upload, record the submission ID, timestamp, and score exactly as shown on the submission-page receipt in ledger/submissions.csv. Do not infer an organizer score from local holdout results.

The format gates run in-project without a human inspector. Portal authentication, quota/slot selection, upload, and receipt capture still require authorized competition-account access; this repository does not automate those actions.

Research queue: unvalidated ideas, not submission candidates

The current protocol's public full-footprint witness blocks all nonempty predictions under its literal overlap rule. Therefore none of these ideas is viable for emission until the policy conflict is resolved. Their relative expected leaderboard DTI cannot be estimated from current evidence; the priority below is screening order/cost, not a score forecast.

  1. Paired DEM scarp-profile asymmetry: pinned DEM/elevation layers (Bands 12 and 19); detect a coherent break with a repeated one-sided vertical offset across profiles, unlike the already-tested slope-break coherence alone. Potentially catches subtle scarps absent from a potential-field/catalogue skeleton. Mimics include terrace risers and fan margins. In-stack; moderate implementation/screen cost; not tested.
  2. Drainage-crossing scarp discrimination: DEM Bands 12/19 plus an official hydrography layer; downweight linear breaks aligned with channels and favor breaks crossing drainage consistently. Could separate tectonic scarps from channel banks, but channel incision and road cuts remain mimics. Requires external data; USGS National Hydrography Dataset availability was not checked in this review, so this is not a viable experiment yet.
  3. Shoreline-conditioned breakline suppression: DEM Bands 12/19 plus mapped Lake Lahontan shoreline elevations/shoreline geometry; suppress contour-parallel risers at known stillstands while retaining transverse scarps. Could reduce a named non-fault mimic, not independently identify faults. Requires external official shoreline data; exact free dataset and obtainability were not verified here, so not viable yet.
  4. Spring/structure strike alignment: existing mapped spring locations plus DEM/structural lineament orientation; test whether spring pairs align along independently detected structures rather than simply placing near vents. Springs can reflect topography and lithology without faults. GDR/INGENIOUS is a listed source, but current data availability and reuse terms were not checked; not viable yet.

These are research questions, not claims of novelty across every GEMSDOE site. See the existing hypothesis inventory and known blockers before proposing a lane.
