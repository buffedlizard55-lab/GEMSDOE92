4. PhD-level note — why GEMSDOE32's h33-h33-2-b2 scored 0.2778, and can we beat it?

Why 0.2778. h33-h33-2-b2-20261004T220000Z-e5eb6e7e-zeros is a sparse binary dot field (37,654 dots) placed off the mapped catalogue (0% on catalogue; mean distance ~29 px). The competition metric is a Distance-Weighted Tversky Index (α=0.2 on false positives, β=0.8 on false negatives, 300 m triangular kernel). A sparse, catalogue-avoiding dot field minimises false-positive mass (each dot carries FP weight) while the distance-tolerant kernel awards dots that land within 300 m of any truth pixel. Thinning and excluding cells near mapped faults removes redundant false positives; under a distance-tolerant metric this concentrates credit on hits. That structural choice — sparse + off-catalogue + distance-tolerant — is the plausible reason it out-scored denser or catalogue-hugging entries and became this session's R0 control.

Can we beat it? Not yet demonstrated. The organizer truth (new geothermal faults) is unpublished; no DrivenData auth is available, so no organizer score or leaderboard query is possible. My candidate is unique and format-valid, but its holdout surrogate (0.05164) does not exceed the reported R0 recipe (0.09775) on the figures I can reproduce, and full public-registry uniqueness is pending. A submission slot should not be spent until (1) a candidate beats the R0 holdout recipe on the shared instrument, (2) it passes literal + surface uniqueness against the full public registry, and (3) all 11 format gates pass.
