Scientific Analysis: Why did GEMSDOE32 Score 0.2778 and How to Beat It?

The highest reported score in the GEMSDOE lineage is 0.2778 for h33-h33-2-b2-20261004T220000Z-e5eb6e7e-zeros, while the current leaderboard high score stands at 0.4413 (with earlier snapshots at 0.3195). Understanding this performance gap requires analyzing the mathematical properties of the scoring metric and the underlying structural geology.

1. Distance-Weighted Tversky Metric ($DTI$) Optimization

The competition evaluation metric is the asymmetric distance-weighted Tversky Index:

DTI = TPw / (TPw + α·FPw + β·FNw + ε)

with α = 0.2, β = 0.8, and triangular kernel $k(d) = \max(0, 1 - d/300\text{ m})$ with cutoff radius $R = 300\text{ m}$ (3 pixels on a 100 m grid). This formulation has crucial properties:

  • Asymmetric Penalties (β=0.8 >> α=0.2): False negatives are penalized 4× more heavily than false positives. However, adding redundant dots along the same fault line does not increase $TP_w$ (max-pooled kernel credit), yet directly increases false-positive mass $FP_w$ and total emitted mass $S$.
  • Poisson-Disk Thinning ($r \ge 2.8\text{ px}$): By enforcing a minimum 280 m distance between predicted points, the D2.8 lineage eliminated redundant along-strike mass, increasing the score from 0.1922 (H19-5 raw skeleton) to 0.2600.
  • Hard Catalogue Clearance ($d_{cat} > 2.0\text{ px}$): Because competition organizers mask all known USGS/INGENIOUS faults pixel-exactly from live scoring, any mass emitted on or adjacent to known faults yields zero true positive credit while paying full false-positive penalty. The B2/R0 step enforced strict clearance, boosting the score to 0.2778.

2. Why 0.2778 Hit an Asymptotic Ceiling

Parent R0 is fundamentally restricted to the H19-5 potential-field worm skeleton (multiscale horizontal gradients of gravity and magnetic anomalies). While potential-field worms effectively capture deep-seated crustal basement faults with substantial density/susceptibility contrasts, they systematically miss:

  • Active Quaternary Scarps in Basin Alluvium: Faults offsetting like-lithologies (alluvium against alluvium) produce sharp topographic scarps visible on high-resolution DEMs (1m LiDAR / 10m DEM) without any gravity or magnetic anomaly.
  • Geothermal Permeability Conduits: Fluid-bearing geothermal vents and stepover pull-aparts often occur on subsidiary extensional fracture networks offset from deep regional fault cores.
  • Concealed Fault Strands & Relay Jogs: Blind tips, stepover transfer zones, and cross-faults where strain is actively localized.

3. What Separates 0.2778 (CLAIM) from 0.4413 (leaderboard, fetched 2026-10-24)

Measured in this session (analysis): the owner's climb 0.1922 → 0.2778 is explained entirely by emission geometry on a fixed skeleton (dot spacing → 3 px kernel radius; catalogue-adjacent mass 14.3% → 0%; deleting 69% of pixels cost only 4.5 points of kernel coverage). Geometry is saturated. The remaining gap must therefore be skeleton content — fault traces the H19-5 worm skeleton never contained. The leading hypothesis (labelled hypothesis, not measured here) is that top teams exploit the organizer-distributed 1 m DEM coverage — the experts who made the labels mapped manually, most plausibly on high-resolution topography. That dataset requires DrivenData login and is the project's largest access gap. Within the 100 m stack, seven off-skeleton channels have now been tested in this repo family (BREAKLINE, SCARPPOL, ANASIG, STRAINBREAK, gravity crests, STEPEDGE, radiometric concordance screened); none has an organizer receipt, so none can claim to beat 0.2778.
