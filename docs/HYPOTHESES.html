Hypothesis Generation & Ranking Methodology

To maximize the probability of winning (Maximize P(Win)), each candidate hypothesis is evaluated against strict scientific and statistical criteria prior to allocating precious weekly competition slots:

  • Specific Layer Identification: Clear attribution to sensor bands (magnetics, gravity, geodesy, topography, radiometrics).
  • Physical Signature & Transformation: Mathematical edge detection, structure tensors, directional gradients, or curvature transforms.
  • Missing Fault Rationale: Explicit geological mechanism explaining why the feature is absent from legacy catalogues (USGS QFaults / INGENIOUS) but detectable by expert DEM mappers and geothermal explorers.
  • Distinctness from Existing Approaches: Clear geometric and algorithmic separation from prior on-skeleton and near-catalogue methods.
  • Falsification & Non-Fault Mimic: Named non-tectonic geological or geomorphic processes (e.g. shoreline terraces, fan boundaries, lithologic contacts) that could mimic the signature.
  • Leakage Canary & Holdout Validation: Single-feature AUC < 0.90 canary check followed by spatially blocked hide-and-recover holdout evaluation.

1. BREAKLINE — Structure-Tensor Coherence on Detrended Slope Breaks

Layers Involved
Band 19 (det_elev_slope) & Band 12 (det_elev) from training_features.tif
Physical Signature
L = pct_rank(||grad det_elev_slope||) × structure_tensor_coherence(det_elev; σd=1, σt=2)
Why Missing from Catalogue
Faults offsetting like-lithologies in basin alluvium or young volcanic deposits create distinct, linear slope breaks on DEMs without generating potential-field (gravity/magnetic) contrasts. Potential-field worms miss them, but expert DEM mappers trace them directly.
Distinction from Prior Lanes
Sources dots from coherent DEM breaks >3 px away from the potential-field worm skeleton (H19-5), breaking out of the 99.4% skeleton duplication trap.
Named Non-Fault Mimic
Pleistocene Lake Lahontan shoreline terraces, alluvial-fan segment boundaries, channel banks, and road cuts.
Screen & Holdout Result
HOLDOUT-DTI: 0.004861 [0.004032, 0.005822] (Δ vs R0 = +0.000854); SGMC off-catalogue control +31% over R0; Canary Max AUC = 0.6216. Local uniqueness: worst forward overlap 55.0% vs R0.
Data Source
In-stack (USGS 3DEP derived DEM bands); official link: USGS 3DEP

Rank 2 · High Priority · In-Stack Stack Analysis

2. GAMMA-CREST — Signed Gravity Horizontal-Gradient Zero-Crossing / Offset Ridge

Layers Involved
Band 18 (iso_grav_anom_hg, signed horizontal gradient) & Band 5 (iso_grav_anom_slope)
Physical Signature
Zero-crossing and asymmetric peak crests of signed isostatic gravity horizontal gradient, tracking the steepest density step and dip-direction offset.
Why Missing from Catalogue
Basin-bounding normal faults with thick hanging-wall sediment fill often have buried surface traces. The density contrast boundary sits basinward of the mapped bedrock-alluvium contact.
Distinction from Prior Lanes
All prior gravity lanes used unsigned gradient magnitudes; Band 18 is the only signed band, allowing dip-directed basinward offset placement.
Named Non-Fault Mimic
Intra-basin paleotopography, paleochannels, and volcanic flow margins buried under alluvium.
Data Source
In-stack (USGS GeoDAWN gravity survey); official link: USGS GeoDAWN Catalog

Rank 3 · Active Tectonics · Multi-Sensor Coupling

3. DILATATIONAL-STEPOVER — Geodetic Strain Interaction on Structural Relay Bends

Layers Involved
Band 8 (geod_dilaterate), Band 7 (geod_shearrate), Band 4 (geod_2ndinv), combined with Band 19 DEM breaks
Physical Signature
Coupling high positive dilatation rate (volumetric crustal extension) with non-collinear DEM scarp terminations (relay ramps and pull-apart jogs).
Why Missing from Catalogue
Active geothermal fluid conduits localize in dilatational jogs where mean normal stress is lowest and hydraulic conductivity is maximized, often between mapped overlapping fault segments.
Distinction from Prior Lanes
Prior DILATATION lanes only re-ranked points along the static potential-field skeleton; this hypothesis uses strain as an interaction multiplier on off-skeleton structural breaks.
Named Non-Fault Mimic
Broad regional extensional strain gradients lacking localized fault control.
Data Source
In-stack geodetic GPS/InSAR strain inversion (UNR Nevada Geodetic Laboratory / USGS); official link: UNR Geodetic Lab

Rank 4 · Hydrothermal Alteration · Radiometric Fusion

4. HYDROTHERMAL-ALTERATION — Radiometric Potassium/Thorium Depletion/Enrichment Haloes

Layers Involved
External GeoDAWN airborne radiometric survey (Bands: Potassium K, Thorium eTh, Total Count; geodawn_rad_u8.tif)
Physical Signature
Sharp gradients in K/Th ratio coupled with local magnetic destruction (low TMI anomaly) representing hydrothermal fluid alteration along permeable fault corridors.
Why Missing from Catalogue
Hydrothermal alteration halos identify permeable, fluid-transmitting fault segments that may lack significant structural throw or scarp relief.
Distinction from Prior Lanes
Directly targets geochemical fluid-rock interaction rather than purely mechanical relief or potential-field density/susceptibility steps.
Named Non-Fault Mimic
Lithologic transitions (e.g. rhyolitic tuffs vs basaltic flows) and agricultural/evaporite surface deposits in playas.
Data Source
USGS GeoDAWN Airborne Radiometric Survey; official link: USGS GeoDAWN Radiometrics

Rank 5 · Geomorphic Specificity · Shoreline Filtering

5. SHORELINE-FILTERED SCARP — Elevation-Band Suppressed DEM Breaklines

Layers Involved
Band 19 (det_elev_slope), Band 12 (det_elev), and Lake Lahontan highstand contour masks (~1330 m AMSL)
Physical Signature
Suppression of breaks running strictly parallel to topographic contours at known pluvial lake stillstand elevations, isolating cross-contour tectonic scarps.
Why Missing from Catalogue
Removes the dominant non-tectonic false-positive mimic in the Great Basin (wave-cut terraces), increasing signal-to-noise for subtle fault scarps.
Distinction from Prior Lanes
Directly addresses the primary known falsifier of the BREAKLINE lane.
Data Source
USGS 3DEP Elevation + NBMG Lahontan Shoreline Database; official link: Nevada Bureau of Mines and Geology
