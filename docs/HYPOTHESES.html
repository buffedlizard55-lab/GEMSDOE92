DRAINAGE-RELAY — repeated channel deflection and knickpoint geometry

Layers
Competition DEM Band 12 det_elev, Band 19 det_elev_slope, and a derived hydrologically conditioned drainage graph / channel profiles.
Physical signature
Collinear, repeated same-sense lateral channel deflections or knickpoints across neighboring catchments with a consistent cross-channel step. Score a network motif, not one high-gradient pixel.
Why a catalogue-missing fault?
A young, low-throw blind fault may deform several channels before a clear surface scarp is mapped; repetition across catchments may persist when one local break is weak.
Difference from repo work
BREAKLINE and SCARPPOL rank local slope-break / fall-line-curvature pixels. A drainage-graph and cross-catchment displacement test is distinct; no such tracked builder or result was found.
Named non-fault mimics
Lithologic/cementation boundaries; fan avulsion or drainage capture; Pleistocene shorelines; roads, canals or dams.
Expected value / cost
Medium potential information value; moderate-to-high cost. A 100 m DEM may be too coarse for small offsets. No numeric score forecast.
Data and source status
Requires exact authorized DEM bytes and holdout labels. Official competition data page; access/terms not verified and the cache is absent. Not viable now.

SPRING-PAIR-STRIKE — geothermal discharge pair alignment

Layers
DOE GDR INGENIOUS spring/well coordinates, temperatures and chemistry with source-quality flags; optional DEM-derived catchment/valley orientation for controls.
Physical signature
Fault-compatible, strike-coherent pairwise orientations among independent hot-spring or geothermometry-confirmed well points, with spatially blocked controls—not point density.
Why a catalogue-missing fault?
Blind permeable strands may connect separated discharge points even when the connecting fault lacks a mapped Quaternary scarp.
Difference from repo work
A related spring-density/elongation screen was not promising. Pairwise strike alignment was explicitly left unbuilt; this is not a claim that the density screen worked.
Named non-fault mimics
Aquifer or lithologic-contact discharge; rivers/playa margins; anthropogenic wells; multiple outlets fed by one aquifer rather than a fault.
Expected value / cost
Medium-high geological specificity but uncertain power; moderate-high cost for provenance, duplicate removal, cluster dependence and leakage-safe validation. No numeric score forecast.
Data and source status
Official GDR / INGENIOUS #1391. The project manifest records a CC BY 4.0 classification for a pinned mirror, but this review did not verify the live source or current terms. Not viable now.

Rank 3 · low-to-medium information value · high cost

HOST-NORMALIZED-RADIOMETRIC-RESIDUAL — multi-channel alteration edge

Layers
USGS GeoDAWN K, eTh, eU and total count; competition Band 15 depth_to_base_surf and Band 17 cond_surf as candidate host/cover covariates; survey-quality masks.
Physical signature
Host-conditioned local multi-channel residuals with coherent linear K/eTh/eU alteration edges; not a raw total-count gradient or unconditioned ratio.
Why a catalogue-missing fault?
Fluid-rock alteration may mark a concealed permeable fault or splay with little relief and weak magnetic/gravity contrast.
Difference from repo work
A related total-count-gradient screen exists. The proposed host-normalized multichannel residual is a distinct but unvalidated formulation; radiometric work generally is not untouched.
Named non-fault mimics
Felsic/mafic lithology; soil moisture and transported cover; playa salts/clay; flight-line striping, altitude or shielding.
Expected value / cost
Low-to-medium until host normalization and survey QA are demonstrated; high cost for co-registration and independent controls. No numeric score forecast.
Data and source status
USGS GeoDAWN catalog · DOI 10.5066/P93LGLVQ. The manifest records a public-domain classification for a pinned mirror; live source, coverage and availability were not verified. Not viable now.

Rank 4 · low-to-medium potential · high cost

MULTISEASON-THERMAL-CORRIDOR — persistent surface-temperature residual

Layers
Multi-date Landsat Collection 2 surface temperature; DEM slope/aspect, land cover and acquisition-quality controls; optional independently sourced springs for interpretation, never target labels.
Physical signature
Persistent, seasonally matched thermal residuals along a narrow corridor after controlling for slope/aspect, land cover, acquisition time and emissivity; require repeated scenes.
Why a catalogue-missing fault?
Shallow geothermal upflow may produce persistent surface heat above a blind permeable structure absent from scarp and potential-field maps.
Difference from repo work
No Landsat thermal time-series lane was found in the reviewed tracked code/evidence. The thermal relation to fault labels is indirect.
Named non-fault mimics
Sun-facing slopes; irrigation, urban or industrial heat; wildfire; exposed rock/emissivity artifacts; seasonal moisture and atmospheric correction.
Expected value / cost
Low-to-medium because anomalies are indirect; high cost for scene selection, cloud/season controls and reproducible processing. No numeric score forecast.
Data and source status
USGS Landsat Collection 2 Surface Temperature. Source availability, coverage and terms were not checked in this review. Not viable now.
