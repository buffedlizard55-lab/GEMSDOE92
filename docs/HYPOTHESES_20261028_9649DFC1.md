## Candidate specifications and named mimics

### 1. 1 m LiDAR micro-scarp / Lahontan shoreline veto

- **Inputs:** USGS 3DEP 1 m bare-earth DEM where coverage exists; competition-supplied `1m_DEM_links.csv` only after its origin and terms are authenticated; independent orthometric elevation / shoreline context if available.
- **Transform:** derive multiscale cross-scarp curvature and paired slope-breaks, measure continuity and orientation, then suppress shoreline-parallel beach ridges and roads before 100 m aggregation. Avoid treating resampling as new spatial detail.
- **Fault rationale:** young or reactivated blind faults may produce decimeter-to-meter relief that is smoothed in the existing 10 m terrain features.
- **Named non-fault mimics:** Lake Lahontan wave-cut beach ridges, fan-head scarps, road cuts and channel banks.
- **Official starting points:** [USGS 3DEP](https://www.usgs.gov/3d-elevation-program) and [USGS 1 m DEM catalog](https://data.usgs.gov/datacatalog/data/USGS:77ae0551-c61e-4979-aedd-d797abdcde0e). Linked for later review; neither AOI coverage nor download/access was verified in this session.

### 2. ASTER TIR silica / Landsat SWIR alteration seam

- **Inputs:** ASTER Global Emissivity Database (TIR) and Landsat Collection 2 surface-reflectance SWIR, plus the existing geology, terrain, MT and radiometric layers after provenance checks.
- **Transform:** calculate silica/chalcedony emissivity and clay/OH absorption indicators with QA masks, then test lineament-scale continuity and orientation against independent structural evidence. Use explicit bare ground and lithology controls.
- **Fault / vent rationale:** silicified and argillic alteration can mark hydrothermal fluid pathways near buried structures even where a surface scarp or active spring is absent; alteration is supporting evidence, not proof of a fault.
- **Named non-fault mimics:** quartz-rich dunes or talus, unaltered felsic lithology, mine disturbance and mixed pixels.
- **Official starting points:** [NASA LP DAAC ASTER GED v3](https://lpdaac.usgs.gov/products/ag100v003/) and [USGS Landsat Collection 2](https://www.usgs.gov/landsat-missions/landsat-collection-2-level-2-science-products). Links and products are not live-verified here; AOI coverage, scene quality and exact usage terms require checking.

### 3. Repeat-pass thermal persistence

- **Inputs:** multi-date USGS Landsat Collection 2 Level-2 Surface Temperature; ECOSTRESS observations only if temporal coverage and cloud/quality metadata are adequate; existing DEM, aspect, land cover, irrigation and thermal-well context.
- **Transform:** estimate date/season-specific temperature residuals after atmospheric and land-surface controls; require repeatable linear anomalies and quantify their alignment with independent structures rather than selecting single hot pixels.
- **Fault / vent rationale:** sustained shallow heat-flow or groundwater leakage can trace permeable fault zones below alluvium.
- **Named non-fault mimics:** irrigated agriculture, wildfire scars, exposed dark rock, urban/industrial heat, solar aspect and cold-air pooling.
- **Official starting points:** [USGS Landsat Collection 2 Surface Temperature](https://www.usgs.gov/landsat-missions/landsat-collection-2-surface-temperature) and [NASA/JPL ECOSTRESS](https://ecostress.jpl.nasa.gov/). Linked only; no scene inventory or time-series availability was verified.

### 4. Groundwater head / temperature phase-lag barriers

- **Inputs:** USGS NWIS continuous or discrete groundwater level and, only where measured with adequate QA, temperature/conductivity; well construction, screen depth and pumping metadata; mapped aquifer/lithology.
- **Transform:** remove seasonal and pumping effects; compare phase lag, amplitude attenuation and head gradients between nearby wells on opposite sides of candidate structures. Do not turn a sparse station network into a raster without a spatially blocked validation design.
- **Fault / vent rationale:** a low-permeability fault core can create a hydraulic-head or seasonal-response discontinuity; a damage zone may instead act as a preferential conduit. Direction of effect is site-dependent.
- **Named non-fault mimics:** pumping/reinjection, aquifer pinch-outs, clay-rich stratigraphic contacts and well-screen differences.
- **Official starting point:** [USGS Water Data / NWIS](https://waterdata.usgs.gov/nwis) and [Water Services API documentation](https://waterservices.usgs.gov/rest/IV-Service.html). Station coverage, specific parameter availability and API reachability were not verified.

### 5. Event-specific focal mechanisms

- **Inputs:** USGS ComCat/ANSS events with reported focal mechanisms and uncertainty, plus existing geodetic strain and geophysical-edge orientation. Exclude or separately model aftershock and induced sequences.
- **Transform:** represent nodal planes as axial orientation distributions, propagate uncertainty, and test coherent spatial alignment with the local strain tensor and independent basement structure. Do not count two nodal planes as two independent faults.
- **Fault rationale:** clusters of compatible event-specific slip planes can outline active concealed strands that are poorly represented by surface scarps.
- **Named non-fault mimics:** induced geothermal swarms, volcanic/dike sequences, aftershocks on mapped faults and poorly constrained small events.
- **Official starting points:** [USGS FDSN event service](https://earthquake.usgs.gov/fdsnws/event/1/) and [ComCat documentation](https://earthquake.usgs.gov/data/comcat/). Event-mechanism coverage and API reachability were not checked.
