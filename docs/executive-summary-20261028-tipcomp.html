Per-site verdicts (focus 9)

Common finding: every focus-9 peak is a dotted/pruned surgery of the same GEMSDOE19 H19-5 skeleton — 87–100% of each peak's dots lie within 3 px of R0 — so no peak is lane-unique and none should be resubmitted. Inside each site, its peak is nevertheless its best file:

  • GEMSDOE36 (0.2750) — also 0.0% ≤2 px; effectively an R0 twin (97.4% near R0). Second-best CLAIM, not unique.
  • GEMSDOE28 (0.2710) — four near-tied rungs (0.2649–0.2710); h36-1 is its best. 6.2% of dots still ≤2 px explains the −0.007 gap to R0.
  • GEMSDOE31 (0.2708) — solo D2.8 with 100 m collar; same family as GEMSDOE28's solo rung.
  • GEMSDOE33 (0.2632) — tip/stepover-augmented D2.8; augmentation did not beat the plain 200 m collar.
  • GEMSDOE25 = GEMSDOE30 (0.2600) — identical dot support (verified): D2.8 without collar (14.6% ≤2 px). One file, two sites.
  • GEMSDOE24 (0.2477) — denser D1.5 dotting: more recall, more FP; the 1−s/12 thinning law favours s=2.8.
  • GEMSDOE27 (0.2449) — gap-closure on D1.5 inherits D1.5's FP drag; its later CELF files (0.219–0.225) score lower.

Why 0.2778 won, and can we beat it? R0 optimally thins a good skeleton (76.7% on-trace kernel recall at 1/2.8 the FP cost) and empties the proven 100–200 m dead collar — but it spends all 37,654 dots on that one skeleton. The 128-claim inversion says truth density is ×5.65 beyond 1.5 km from the catalogue, ×3.73 one pixel off the H19-5 centreline, and ×15.4 where ≥5 independent off-H19 detectors agree. TIPCOMP reallocates along exactly those gradients (plus catalogue tip-continuations and kernel-coverage gaps), which is why both MODEL specs put it at 0.31–0.35. Reaching 0.4413 needs sub-100 m localisation (1 m USGS 3DEP LiDAR — hypothesis H-92F5, deferred: host unreachable from this sandbox).
